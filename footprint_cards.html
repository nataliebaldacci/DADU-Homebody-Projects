DADU Footprint Cards

Detailed building footprint data for every Nashville property

What is a Footprint Card?

A footprint card is a detailed summary of a building's physical footprint on a parcel. It provides key metrics about structures on the property, helping homeowners, contractors, and designers understand existing conditions before planning a DADU. Each card pulls from Nashville's assessor records, building footprint data, and ArcGIS spatial layers to give you an accurate picture of what already exists on the lot.

What a Footprint Card Shows

Every footprint card contains six essential data points about structures on a parcel.

Structure Type

Primary residence, garage, shed, or existing accessory structure. Each building on the parcel is classified individually.

Building Area

Square footage of each structure's ground-floor footprint, measured from assessor records and spatial data.

Height

Measured height of existing structures. Under BL2025-1007, a DADU cannot exceed the height of the principal structure.

Year Built

Construction year from assessor records, useful for understanding the age and condition of existing improvements.

Roof Type

Roof style and material classification. Helpful for designers planning a DADU that complements existing architecture.

Lot Coverage

Percentage of the lot covered by existing structures. A critical factor in determining how much buildable area remains.

How to Use Footprint Data

Footprint cards serve different purposes depending on your role in a DADU project.

Homeowners

Understand what is already on your lot before planning a DADU addition. See how much buildable area remains and what your maximum DADU footprint can be.

Contractors

Verify existing structures and lot coverage before bidding on a project. Accurate footprint data helps you scope work and estimate costs more precisely.

Designers

Use footprint dimensions for site plans and setback calculations. Knowing existing structure locations and sizes is essential for code-compliant designs.
