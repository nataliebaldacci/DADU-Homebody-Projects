Required for All Detached Structures

Restrictive covenants must be filed with Davidson County before building any detached structure in Nashville. This includes DADUs, garages, sheds, and any accessory building with plumbing, HVAC, or electrical permits.

What is a Restrictive Covenant?

A restrictive covenant is a legal document recorded with your property deed that limits how a structure can be used. For detached structures, it ensures the building remains accessory to your primary residence and cannot be converted into an independent dwelling without proper permits. The covenant stays with the property and transfers to future owners.

Key Facts

File Before Permit

The covenant must be recorded with the Register of Deeds before your building permit can be issued by Metro Codes.

Stays with Property

The covenant is permanently attached to your deed and automatically transfers to any future property owners.

Register of Deeds

File at 501 Broadway, Suite 501, Nashville, TN 37203. The recording fee is $12 for standard documents.

Email to Codes

After recording, email the stamped copy to permitissuance@nashville.gov to release your building permit.

What Does the Covenant Restrict?

The restrictive covenant ensures the DADU remains accessory to the primary dwelling. It cannot be subdivided or sold separately from the main property. If the primary dwelling is demolished, the DADU cannot be used as the sole dwelling on the lot without additional permits and zoning approval.
